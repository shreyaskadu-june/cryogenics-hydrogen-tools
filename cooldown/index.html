Sources, method and limits

  • Cooldown time (NBS 9264): Brennan, Brentari, Smith and Steward, "An Experimental Report on Cooldown of Cryogenic Transfer Lines", NBS Report 9264 (NASA, 1967). Cooldown time is the low-temperature knee at 60.4 m from the supply dewar. Figure 8 gives hydrogen cooldown time against driving pressure for the 61 m, 15.9 mm bore copper line. Values were read from the figure and are approximate (about ±5 s). Times run from about 145 s at 2.5 atm to about 33 s at 11.1 atm.
  • Calibration: the utilisation factor is calibrated to the 30–50 L consumption quoted for this line in secondary literature (the SINDA/FLUINT paper). The NBS 9264 text does not give a liquid volume, and the flow figure that might give it was not read, so this is not verified against the primary report. The model gives 0.92 kg (13 L) of ideal use, so the implied useful share is 26–43%.
  • Copper heat capacity: NIST Cryogenic Materials Database, OFHC copper curve fit (4–300 K; 10% error below 15 K, 5% above).
  • Hydrogen enthalpy: NIST via CoolProp. From saturated liquid at 20.4 K to gas at 300 K, 1 bar: about 3,958 kJ/kg.
  • Not modelled: stainless or other pipe materials, supply and exhaust plena, vapour blow-off dynamics, two-phase flow and chill-down, insulation heat leak, and any pressure drop or temperature gradient along the line. The NBS study found pressure drop and friction to be the biggest sources of uncertainty, which this page does not model.
  • Uncertainty: the NBS study recommends a safety factor of at least 1.3 on time and cryogen quantity. The model's utilisation factor is only calibrated to one test, so treat results as screening estimates.
