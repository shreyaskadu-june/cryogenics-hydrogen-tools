How fast does a liquid hydrogen tank lose its contents?

Set the tank size, how full it is, how well it is insulated and the outside temperature. The page works out the heat leaking in, the hydrogen that boils off each day, and how the liquid level falls over time. Boil-off is vented to the atmosphere, so the tank stays at about 1 bar.

Tank

Default is the 175 ft³ (4.96 m³) tank in the NASA 1991 test.

Insulation and outside

1.0 matches the NASA multilayer insulation test. Below 1 assumes better insulation; above 1 assumes worse.

Limits follow the NASA test range (152 °R to 630 °R).

Model: heat leak per m² of tank surface, taken from the three NASA test points and interpolated; area scales with volume to the two-thirds power (similar shape). Boil-off = heat leak ÷ latent heat of 448.7 kJ/kg at 20.4 K.

Heat input matches NASA at the three test points by construction, so this table checks the scaling only. At 294 K and 350 K the model's loss percentage is 14–18% higher than NASA's reported figure, which uses a basis NASA does not state. At 84 K NASA's 0.2% is rounded to one decimal, so the gap there is within rounding. The page reports the model's own figure and does not adjust it to match.

Sources, method and limits

  • Heat leak and loss: Stochl and Knoll, "Thermal Performance of a Liquid Hydrogen Tank Multilayer Insulation System at Warm Boundary Temperatures of 630, 530 and 152 °R", NASA TM 104476 (AIAA-91-2400, 1991). Tank 175 ft³, aluminium, 34 layers of MLI at about 4×10⁻⁶ torr. Heat inputs 164.4, 95.8 and 15.9 BTU/hr (48.2, 28.1, 4.7 kW converted at 1 BTU/hr = 0.29307 W).
  • Latent heat and liquid density: NIST Standard Reference Database 12 via CoolProp: 448.7 kJ/kg and 70.85 kg/m³ at 1 bar saturation (20.37 K).
  • Tank geometry: the NASA tank is an oblate spheroid, 87.6 in equatorial and 73.0 in polar diameter. Surface area 13.9 m² (computed).
  • Not modelled: pressure rise in a sealed tank, relief valves, sloshing, liquid heating and ortho-para conversion, the heat leak through supports and piping of a real plant, and insulation that has aged or been damaged. The 1991 MLI data is a single test, and modern insulation may differ.
  • Uncertainty: the NASA test is for one tank. The page does not add an uncertainty band; treat the outputs as a screening estimate within roughly ±20%, based on the 14–18% gap in the check table.
