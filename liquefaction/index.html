How much energy does it take to liquefy hydrogen?

Set a plant's output and how close it runs to the theoretical minimum. The page shows the energy per kilogram, the daily and yearly electricity use, and how the result compares with the minimum and with today's large industrial liquefiers.

Plant

Range follows the DOE model (6,000 to 200,000 kg/day). The 27,000 kg/day default is an example, not a recommendation.

Default 19% gives 15 kWh/kg, the middle of DOE's 10–20 kWh/kg range for today's large liquefiers.

Model: energy per kilogram = theoretical minimum ÷ share achieved. Daily and yearly energy follow from output. Plant size is not used to change the efficiency, because the DOE source gives no values for that effect.

Sources, method and limits

  • Theoretical minimum: 2.88 kWh/kg, a reversible Carnot process from a 20 bar inlet, from HDSAM v3.1 as cited in DOE Hydrogen Program document 19001 (hydrogen liquefaction costs). The document gives no date for this value.
  • Industrial range: "in the range of 10–20 kWh/kg" for today's large-scale industrial liquefiers, DOE document 19001. The document gives no plant capacity or year for this range.
  • Plant output range: DOE document 19001 models plant sizes from 6,000 to 200,000 kg/day.
  • Not modelled: plant size effects on efficiency (the source describes a decrease with scale but gives no values), ortho-para conversion energy, refrigerant cycle choice (Claude, Brayton, mixed refrigerant), boil-off during storage, and liquefier capital cost.
  • Uncertainty: the 10–20 kWh/kg range is wide, and the document gives no year or plant basis for it. Treat outputs as screening estimates, and check the figure against a current source before use.
